How to invoice Thai clients

Thai companies often expect invoices that look and add up a certain way: VAT shown separately, withholding tax deducted, and the total written out in Thai words. Here is what that means and how to get it right.

1. VAT 7%

Businesses registered for VAT in Thailand charge 7% VAT and must issue a tax invoice (ใบกำกับภาษี). Registration is required once annual revenue exceeds 1.8 million THB. If you are not VAT-registered, you must not charge VAT. Issue a regular invoice and receipt instead.

2. Withholding tax (usually 3% for services)

When a Thai company pays for services, it commonly withholds tax at source, often 3% for service fees, and pays it to the Revenue Department on your behalf. It is calculated on the amount before VAT.

Ask the client for the withholding tax certificate, known as the "50 Tawi" (50 ทวิ). It proves the tax was paid and can be credited against your Thai income tax. Rates and rules differ for non-residents and for payments made abroad, so check your own situation.

3. Amount in Thai words

Thai invoices and receipts usually show the total in words, for example 17,160 THB = "หนึ่งหมื่นเจ็ดพันหนึ่งร้อยหกสิบบาทถ้วน". Use our free baht-to-Thai-words converter, or let BahtDoc add it automatically in both Thai and English.

4. Bilingual documents

Many Thai accounting teams prefer Thai labels, while you may prefer English. A bilingual invoice (Thai + English labels on the same document) keeps both sides happy and speeds up approval.

5. Get paid faster with PromptPay

PromptPay is Thailand's instant transfer system. A PromptPay QR code with the exact amount lets your client scan and pay from any Thai banking app with no typing errors.
